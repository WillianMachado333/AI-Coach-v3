Lesson 1.1 · Trait Awareness

Section 1 of 8 · Understanding Traits, Skills, and Behaviors · 4 min read

What a trait actually is

Traits are patterns in how you tend to think, feel, communicate, and respond. They influence your natural preferences across many situations.

A trait is a tendency, not a guaranteed outcome. You can manage how your traits appear through awareness, skills, and deliberate choices.

Training may not quickly change your personality. However, experience and reflection can shape traits gradually and help you use them more effectively.

Common misconceptions to watch for

  • Misconception: Traits never change. Correction: Traits are relatively stable, but they may shift over time and can be expressed differently.
  • Misconception: A trait predicts behavior in every situation. Correction: Skills, goals, pressure, expectations, and context also shape behavior.

Observable behaviors — what "getting this" looks like

  • Describing a trait using words such as "tends to" or "often".
  • Explaining that a person can manage how a trait is expressed.
  • Comparing how the same tendency may help in one setting and create risk in another.
  • Avoiding treating an assessment result as a complete description of a person.

Try this — reflection question

Think of one trait you tend to be judged for. Then answer:

  1. In which situation does this trait help you?
  2. In which situation does the same trait become a risk?
Ask your coach to talk through your answer — she can help you notice when the trait is serving you and when it is getting in the way.
