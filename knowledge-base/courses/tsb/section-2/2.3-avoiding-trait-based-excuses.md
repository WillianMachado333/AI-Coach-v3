# Understanding Traits, Skills, and Behaviors — 2.3 Avoiding Trait-Based Excuses

_Section 2: Recognizing Personal Tendencies_

## Lesson (course text)

Traits may explain why some responses feel natural. They do not remove your responsibility for how you behave at work.

Avoid statements such as, “That is just who I am.” Instead, identify skills that can help you respond more effectively.

Accept feedback without using personality as a defense. You may keep the same tendency while learning to express it in a more professional way.

## Framework overview

Traits may help explain why certain responses feel natural, but they do not remove responsibility for behavior. People can still build skills and choose more effective actions.

## Competency Definition

Building skills that improve behavior

## Competency Statement

The ability to identify, practice, and apply skills that support more effective behavior across workplace situations. Workplace Relevance Employers value people who can learn, adapt, and improve. Skill development supports communication, customer service, collaboration, leadership, emotional regulation, problem-solving, and career growth. It also helps coaching and training efforts focus on practical, observable improvement.

## Knowledge

- explanation is different from excuse
- people remain responsible for actions
- skills can help manage tendencies
- accountability includes repair and improvement

## Skills

- acknowledge impact
- accept relevant feedback
- identify a better response
- select a skill or support

## Observable Behaviors

- Owns behavior without blaming personality
- Listens to feedback
- Names an improvement action
- Follows through after a mistake

## I Demonstrate This When

- I do not use my personality as an excuse.
- I take responsibility for how I act.
- I look for skills that help me respond better.
- I accept that I can improve my behavior.

## Performance Indicators

- **Limited**: Needs prompting to demonstrate maintain accountability and may apply it inaccurately or inconsistently.
- **Developing**: Uses traits to defend behavior.
- **Effective**: Acknowledges behavior and identifies a next step.
- **Consistent**: Repairs impact and builds a plan that prevents repetition.

## Examples

- An employee says, “I tend to speak quickly, and I need to pause so others can contribute.”
- A learner apologizes for a dismissive comment and practices a better response.

## Non-Examples

- Saying, “That is just how I am.”
- Agreeing with feedback but making no change.

## Common Misconceptions

- Accountability is not self-criticism; it is ownership and action.
- Understanding a tendency can increase choice rather than reduce responsibility.

## Conversation Starters

- Understanding: How can trait awareness help me without becoming an excuse?
- Reflection or Application: How could I take responsibility in a situation where my natural reaction took over?
- Practice or Improvement: What skill could help me manage a tendency that sometimes causes problems?
- FfTT-TSB 3: Developing Skills for Behavioral Change
- Skills help people respond more effectively, even when their underlying traits remain the same. A person may still be introverted, direct, anxious, cautious, or fast-paced, but they can learn skills that improve communication, decision-making, teamwork, and self-management.
- Problem Statement
- People may believe they cannot change because their traits feel stable, or they may expect change without practicing the skills needed to behave differently.
- Value Statement
- Skill development gives people practical tools to improve behavior, adapt to situations, and strengthen workplace performance.
