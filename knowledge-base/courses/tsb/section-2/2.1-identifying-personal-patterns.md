# Understanding Traits, Skills, and Behaviors — 2.1 Identifying Personal Patterns

_Section 2: Recognizing Personal Tendencies_

## Lesson (course text)

You may repeat patterns in communication, organization, decisions, and emotional reactions. Noticing these patterns is an important part of self-awareness.

Pay attention to how you usually respond under pressure. Notice situations that trigger frustration, avoidance, overconfidence, or uncertainty.

Reflection helps you see habits across work, learning, and relationships. Once you notice a pattern, you can decide whether it supports your goals.

## Framework overview

People often repeat patterns in how they communicate, organize work, handle pressure, and respond to others. Recognizing these patterns is an important first step toward professional growth.

## Competency Definition

Identifying recurring personal responses

## Competency Statement

Recognizes recurring patterns in personal communication, work habits, decision-making, emotional responses, and interactions with others.

## Knowledge

- patterns are identified across several situations
- triggers and conditions can make patterns more likely
- reflection and feedback reveal patterns
- a pattern is different from one isolated event

## Skills

- notice repeated responses
- compare behavior across settings
- identify common triggers
- seek examples from trusted people

## Observable Behaviors

- Records repeated reactions
- Names situations that trigger a usual response
- Compares patterns across settings
- Checks self-observations with feedback

## I Demonstrate This When

- I notice how I usually respond under pressure.
- I recognize patterns in how I communicate.
- I identify situations where I tend to struggle.
- I reflect on habits that affect my performance.

## Performance Indicators

- **Limited**: Needs prompting to demonstrate identify personal patterns and may apply it inaccurately or inconsistently.
- **Developing**: Names isolated events without seeing a pattern.
- **Effective**: Identifies a recurring pattern and its common conditions.
- **Consistent**: Uses several sources of evidence and revises conclusions when needed.

## Examples

- A learner notices they delay asking questions until a deadline is close.
- An employee sees that they become very brief when workload rises.

## Non-Examples

- Calling one mistake a pattern.
- Assuming a pattern is permanent.

## Common Misconceptions

- Patterns can be noticed without blaming yourself.
- A pattern is useful information, not a fixed identity.

## Conversation Starters

- Understanding: How can I tell whether something is a real pattern or a one-time event?
- Reflection or Application: What pattern might be affecting a situation I keep experiencing?
- Practice or Improvement: What is a simple way to track one of my patterns this week?
