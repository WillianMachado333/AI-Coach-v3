# Understanding Traits, Skills, and Behaviors — 4.1 Recognizing Situational Influences

_Section 4: Understanding Behavior in Context_

## Lesson (course text)

Behavior is influenced by the situation. Deadlines, stress, expectations, authority, culture, and relationships can affect how someone responds.

Before judging an action, consider what was happening. A person may communicate differently when confused, unsupported, pressured, or uncertain.

Context does not excuse harmful behavior. It helps you understand possible causes and choose a fairer, more effective response.

## Framework overview

Situations can strongly affect behavior. Recognizing these influences helps people understand why behavior may change across settings.

## Competency Definition

Recognizing how context affects behavior

## Competency Statement

Identifies situational factors that may influence how a person behaves in a specific moment or environment.

## Knowledge

- context can enable or constrain behavior
- power and role affect communication
- stress and uncertainty affect responses
- culture influences expectations

## Skills

- scan the situation
- identify pressures and supports
- consider role and power
- ask what changed

## Observable Behaviors

- Names relevant conditions
- Considers both personal and situational factors
- Checks assumptions
- Avoids using context to erase impact

## I Demonstrate This When

- I consider what was happening in the situation.
- I recognize that pressure can affect behavior.
- I think about expectations before judging actions.
- I look beyond the behavior alone.

## Performance Indicators

- **Limited**: Needs prompting to demonstrate recognize situational influences and may apply it inaccurately or inconsistently.
- **Developing**: Ignores context.
- **Effective**: Identifies several plausible influences.
- **Consistent**: Weighs evidence and keeps accountability in view.

## Examples

- A quiet employee speaks less when senior leaders enter the room.
- A normally organized worker misses steps during an unexpected system outage.

## Non-Examples

- Assuming context excuses harmful behavior.
- Inventing a situational explanation without evidence.

## Common Misconceptions

- Context helps explain behavior but does not automatically justify it.
- The same context may affect people differently.

## Conversation Starters

- Understanding: What kinds of situational factors can change how someone behaves?
- Reflection or Application: What context may have shaped a recent behavior that surprised me?
- Practice or Improvement: How can I ask about context without making excuses for the behavior?
