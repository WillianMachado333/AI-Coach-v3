# Understanding Traits, Skills, and Behaviors — 8.1 Setting Behavior-Based Development Goals

_Section 8: Using Trait, Skill, and Behavior Awareness for Growth_

## Lesson (course text)

Strong development goals describe what you will do differently. Observable actions make progress easier to practice, discuss, and measure.

Connect each goal to a real workplace expectation and identify situations where you will use the new behavior.

Define what improvement will look like. Replace vague goals about changing personality with specific actions you can control and repeat.

## Framework overview

Strong development goals focus on what a person will actually do differently. This makes growth more visible, coachable, and measurable.

## Competency Definition

Setting observable improvement goals

## Competency Statement

Creates development goals that focus on specific, observable behaviors rather than vague personality change.

## Knowledge

- effective goals name an action
- context and frequency improve clarity
- goals should connect to a meaningful outcome
- progress needs evidence

## Skills

- write an observable action
- identify the situation
- define a realistic measure
- review and revise the goal

## Observable Behaviors

- Uses action language
- Names where the behavior will occur
- Defines evidence of progress
- Connects the goal to a purpose

## I Demonstrate This When

- I set goals based on what I will do.
- I avoid vague goals like “change my personality.”
- I identify where I will practice the behavior.
- I define how I will know I am improving.

## Performance Indicators

- **Limited**: Needs prompting to demonstrate set behavior-based goals and may apply it inaccurately or inconsistently.
- **Developing**: Writes a vague trait goal.
- **Effective**: Creates a specific practical goal.
- **Consistent**: Builds a challenging but realistic goal and revises it using evidence.

## Examples

- “In weekly meetings, I will summarize one person’s point before sharing my view.”
- “For the next month, I will notify my team at least one day before a deadline is at risk.”

## Non-Examples

- “Become more confident.”
- “Change my personality.”

## Common Misconceptions

- Behavior-based goals can support deeper growth without defining identity.
- A useful measure does not need to be a formal score.

## Conversation Starters

- Understanding: Why are behavior-based goals easier to act on than personality goals?
- Reflection or Application: How could I turn one of my growth goals into an observable behavior?
- Practice or Improvement: Can you help me write a realistic goal and decide what progress would look like?
