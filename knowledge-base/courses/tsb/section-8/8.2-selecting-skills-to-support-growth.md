# Understanding Traits, Skills, and Behaviors — 8.2 Selecting Skills to Support Growth

_Section 8: Using Trait, Skill, and Behavior Awareness for Growth_

## Lesson (course text)

Trait awareness can help you choose useful skills. A cautious person may build decision-making skills, while a direct person may practice active listening.

Select skills that support a specific behavior and workplace goal. Avoid choosing learning activities that are interesting but unrelated to your need.

Use coaching, practice, courses, or feedback to strengthen the skill. Targeted development helps you manage tendencies without trying to become someone else.

## Framework overview

Trait awareness can help people choose the right skills to develop. For example, a naturally cautious person may build decision-making skills, while a naturally direct person may build active listening skills.

## Competency Definition

Choosing skills for targeted growth

## Competency Statement

Selects skills that help manage personal tendencies, strengthen performance, and support more effective behavior in specific situations.

## Knowledge

- a goal may require several skills
- priority should reflect the main barrier
- skills should match the context
- resources and coaching support development

## Skills

- map a goal to skills
- choose a priority
- find practice resources
- sequence skill development

## Observable Behaviors

- Names the capability behind the goal
- Selects a focused skill
- Explains the connection
- Uses suitable learning support

## I Demonstrate This When

- I choose skills that help me grow.
- I connect skill development to my tendencies.
- I select learning that supports better behavior.
- I use support resources to strengthen my skills.

## Performance Indicators

- **Limited**: Needs prompting to demonstrate select skills to support growth and may apply it inaccurately or inconsistently.
- **Developing**: Chooses generic training.
- **Effective**: Selects a relevant skill.
- **Consistent**: Builds a coherent sequence of skills and practice opportunities.

## Examples

- For a goal of contributing in meetings, a learner selects preparation and concise speaking skills.
- For a goal of reducing conflict, an employee chooses listening and question-framing skills.

## Non-Examples

- Selecting a skill only because a course is available.
- Trying to build many unrelated skills at once.

## Common Misconceptions

- The most interesting skill is not always the most useful next skill.
- A trait may suggest a skill need but does not prescribe one solution.

## Conversation Starters

- Understanding: How do I connect a behavior goal to the skill I need?
- Reflection or Application: Which skill would have the greatest effect on a goal I am working toward?
- Practice or Improvement: What learning and practice resources could help me build that skill?
