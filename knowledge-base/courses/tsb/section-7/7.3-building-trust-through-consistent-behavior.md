# Understanding Traits, Skills, and Behaviors — 7.3 Building Trust Through Consistent Behavior

_Section 7: Applying Trait, Skill, and Behavior Awareness in Workplace Relationships_

## Lesson (course text)

Trust develops through repeated actions over time. Good intentions matter, but other people experience trust through what you consistently do.

Follow through on commitments, communicate honestly, and act respectfully across situations. Reliable behavior makes your actions easier to predict.

When your behavior falls short, acknowledge it and repair the relationship. Consistent accountability can rebuild trust after a mistake.

## Framework overview

Trust is built through repeated behavior over time. People may have good intentions, but others experience trust through what they consistently see and hear.

## Competency Definition

Using awareness for practical growth

## Competency Statement

The ability to use trait, skill, and behavior awareness to set development goals, build skills, track progress, and apply growth across workplace situations. Workplace Relevance Employers value people who take ownership of their growth and can translate learning into observable behavior. This competency supports coaching, employability, leadership development, performance improvement, career readiness, and lifelong learning.

## Knowledge

- trust depends on patterns
- follow-through matters
- transparency supports predictability
- repair is needed after breaches

## Skills

- make realistic commitments
- communicate changes early
- follow through
- repair trust

## Observable Behaviors

- Keeps commitments
- Provides timely updates
- Acts consistently across people and settings
- Acknowledges and repairs lapses

## I Demonstrate This When

- I do what I say I will do.
- I act respectfully across situations.
- I communicate honestly.
- I rebuild trust when I make a mistake.

## Performance Indicators

- **Limited**: Needs prompting to demonstrate build trust through consistency and may apply it inaccurately or inconsistently.
- **Developing**: Relies on good intentions.
- **Effective**: Shows dependable behavior.
- **Consistent**: Builds systems that make reliability visible and responds promptly to breaches.

## Examples

- A team member warns early that a deadline is at risk and proposes a revised plan.
- A leader applies the same stated process fairly across the team.

## Non-Examples

- Making promises that cannot be kept.
- Expecting one grand gesture to replace consistent action.

## Common Misconceptions

- Trust is not the same as agreement or closeness.
- Consistency should not mean rigidly repeating an ineffective approach.

## Conversation Starters

- Understanding: Why does trust depend more on repeated behavior than good intentions?
- Reflection or Application: Which of my actions currently strengthens or weakens trust?
- Practice or Improvement: What small consistent behavior could I start using to build trust?
