# Understanding Traits, Skills, and Behaviors — 7.1 Understanding Differences in Work Style

_Section 7: Applying Trait, Skill, and Behavior Awareness in Workplace Relationships_

## Lesson (course text)

People may organize, communicate, decide, and respond to pressure differently. These differences can reflect traits, skills, culture, experience, and context.

Avoid assuming your preferred style is always best. A different approach may still produce strong results and support the team.

Adapt collaboration when possible and discuss shared expectations. Respecting different work styles can improve inclusion, communication, and teamwork.

## Framework overview

People work differently because of personality tendencies, learned skills, cultural expectations, past experiences, and situational demands. Understanding these differences supports better teamwork.

## Competency Definition

Recognizing different work styles

## Competency Statement

Recognizes that people may have different work styles, communication preferences, decision-making approaches, and responses to pressure.

## Knowledge

- different approaches can produce value
- preferences are not universal rules
- culture and access affect work practices
- shared outcomes require coordination

## Skills

- ask about preferences
- identify common needs
- adapt collaboration
- set clear agreements

## Observable Behaviors

- Shows curiosity
- Avoids ranking styles without evidence
- Negotiates practical agreements
- Includes different ways of contributing

## I Demonstrate This When

- I recognize that people work differently.
- I avoid judging someone only because their style differs from mine.
- I adapt how I collaborate with others.
- I respect different ways of contributing.

## Performance Indicators

- **Limited**: Needs prompting to demonstrate respect differences in work style and may apply it inaccurately or inconsistently.
- **Developing**: Expects everyone to work the same way.
- **Effective**: Adapts collaboration respectfully.
- **Consistent**: Builds inclusive routines that use complementary strengths.

## Examples

- A team agrees on both written updates and live discussion.
- A manager provides advance agendas while keeping room for spontaneous ideas.

## Non-Examples

- Using “different style” to ignore missed requirements.
- Forcing one person to carry all adaptation.

## Common Misconceptions

- Respecting styles does not remove shared standards.
- A style preference is not a cultural stereotype.

## Conversation Starters

- Understanding: Why do different work styles sometimes create misunderstanding?
- Reflection or Application: How might my preferred style affect someone who works differently?
- Practice or Improvement: What agreement could help two different styles work together better?
