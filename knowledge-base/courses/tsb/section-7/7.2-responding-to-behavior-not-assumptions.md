# Understanding Traits, Skills, and Behaviors — 7.2 Responding to Behavior, Not Assumptions

_Section 7: Applying Trait, Skill, and Behavior Awareness in Workplace Relationships_

## Lesson (course text)

Respond to what happened instead of assuming negative intent. Unsupported assumptions can increase conflict and damage workplace relationships.

Ask clarifying questions when the situation is unclear. Focus on the behavior, its impact, and the expectation that needs attention.

Address concerns directly and respectfully. This approach supports accountability while giving the other person a fair opportunity to explain and improve.

## Framework overview

Effective workplace relationships require responding to what people actually do rather than assuming negative intent or making broad judgments.

## Competency Definition

Addressing actions without assumptions

## Competency Statement

Responds to observed behavior directly and respectfully without relying on unsupported assumptions about motives, character, or intent.

## Knowledge

- intent is often uncertain
- clarifying questions improve accuracy
- impact can be addressed directly
- curiosity and boundaries can coexist

## Skills

- describe the action
- ask a neutral question
- state impact
- request a needed change

## Observable Behaviors

- Addresses a specific event
- Uses questions before conclusions
- Avoids blame language
- Sets clear expectations

## I Demonstrate This When

- I respond to what happened, not what I assume.
- I ask questions before drawing conclusions.
- I focus on impact instead of blame.
- I address concerns respectfully.

## Performance Indicators

- **Limited**: Needs prompting to demonstrate respond to behavior, not assumptions and may apply it inaccurately or inconsistently.
- **Developing**: Acts on assumed intent.
- **Effective**: Clarifies and addresses behavior.
- **Consistent**: Maintains fairness while responding firmly to patterns.

## Examples

- A coworker asks, “I noticed the handoff was not completed. What got in the way?”
- A supervisor discusses repeated interruptions without calling them intentional disrespect.

## Non-Examples

- Ignoring harmful behavior because intent is unknown.
- Pretending all explanations are equally supported.

## Common Misconceptions

- Checking assumptions does not require accepting an implausible explanation.
- Behavior can be addressed even when motive remains unclear.

## Conversation Starters

- Understanding: Why is it risky to assume I know someone’s motive?
- Reflection or Application: What assumption might be shaping how I see a current conflict?
- Practice or Improvement: How can I ask a clarifying question and still be clear about the impact?
