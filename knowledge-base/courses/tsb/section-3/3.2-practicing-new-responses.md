# Understanding Traits, Skills, and Behaviors — 3.2 Practicing New Responses

_Section 3: Developing Skills for Behavioral Change_

## Lesson (course text)

New behavior becomes easier through practice. Rehearsing a response prepares you to use it when a real situation becomes stressful.

You can practice through role play, coaching, reflection, or low-risk conversations. Repetition helps the response feel more natural and confident.

Try different approaches and learn from feedback. Continued practice turns an unfamiliar response into a skill you can use more consistently.

## Framework overview

Behavioral change requires practice. People need opportunities to rehearse new responses before they can use them consistently in real workplace situations.

## Competency Definition

Practicing alternative workplace responses

## Competency Statement

Practices new communication, self-management, decision-making, or interpersonal responses to support more effective behavior.

## Knowledge

- practice should target a clear action
- realistic scenarios improve transfer
- feedback guides adjustment
- repetition supports confidence and fluency

## Skills

- rehearse a response
- use role play or simulation
- request focused feedback
- repeat with increasing difficulty

## Observable Behaviors

- Practices a specific action
- Uses realistic examples
- Adjusts after feedback
- Repeats practice across conditions

## I Demonstrate This When

- I practice a better response before I need it.
- I try new ways of handling difficult situations.
- I use coaching or feedback to improve.
- I keep practicing until I become more confident.

## Performance Indicators

- **Limited**: Needs prompting to demonstrate practice new responses and may apply it inaccurately or inconsistently.
- **Developing**: Talks about change without rehearsal.
- **Effective**: Practices a clear response and improves it.
- **Consistent**: Uses varied scenarios and can explain what changed.

## Examples

- A learner role-plays how to ask for clarification from a supervisor.
- A team member rehearses a calm opening for a difficult conversation.

## Non-Examples

- Memorizing a script without adapting to the listener.
- Practicing only after a serious problem occurs.

## Common Misconceptions

- Practice is not fake; it prepares a person to act intentionally.
- Early practice may feel awkward and still be useful.

## Conversation Starters

- Understanding: Why does practicing a response help when I already know what I should do?
- Reflection or Application: What situation could I rehearse before I face it again?
- Practice or Improvement: Can you help me practice a better response and give focused feedback?
