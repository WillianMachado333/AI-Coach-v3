# Understanding Traits, Skills, and Behaviors — 3.1 Identifying Skill Gaps

_Section 3: Developing Skills for Behavioral Change_

## Lesson (course text)

Behavioral improvement begins with identifying the skill you need. A specific skill gap is easier to address than a vague goal to improve.

Connect difficult situations to possible missing skills. Conflict may reveal a listening gap, while missed deadlines may reveal a planning gap.

Ask trusted people for feedback and prioritize skills connected to workplace expectations. Focus on one useful development need at a time.

## Framework overview

Before people can change behavior, they need to understand which skills are missing or underdeveloped. This helps development planning become more specific and useful.

## Competency Definition

Recognizing skills needed for improvement

## Competency Statement

Identifies skills that need to be developed in order to improve workplace behavior, communication, performance, or relationships.

## Knowledge

- a behavior problem may have several causes
- skill gaps should be specific
- feedback and evidence improve accuracy
- priority depends on impact and opportunity

## Skills

- connect behavior to a skill
- gather evidence
- choose a high-value gap
- write a specific development need

## Observable Behaviors

- Names a specific skill
- Uses examples to support the need
- Checks whether barriers are also present
- Prioritizes one or two skills

## I Demonstrate This When

- I identify the skills I need to improve.
- I connect behavior challenges to skill development.
- I ask for feedback about where I can grow.
- I focus on specific skills rather than vague improvement goals.

## Performance Indicators

- **Limited**: Needs prompting to demonstrate identify skill gaps and may apply it inaccurately or inconsistently.
- **Developing**: Uses vague goals.
- **Effective**: Links a recurring challenge to a specific skill.
- **Consistent**: Distinguishes skill gaps from unclear expectations or missing resources.

## Examples

- A worker links repeated customer misunderstandings to questioning and summarizing skills.
- A student identifies planning as the skill needed to start assignments earlier.

## Non-Examples

- Calling every performance problem a motivation issue.
- Selecting many skills without priorities.

## Common Misconceptions

- Not every problem is a skill gap; resources, expectations, or workload may matter.
- A skill gap is a development target, not a personal flaw.

## Conversation Starters

- Understanding: How can I tell whether a problem comes from a skill gap or something else?
- Reflection or Application: What skill may be missing in a situation where I am not getting the result I want?
- Practice or Improvement: How should I choose which skill gap to work on first?
