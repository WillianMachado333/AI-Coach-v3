# Understanding Traits, Skills, and Behaviors — 3.3 Translating Learning into Action

_Section 3: Developing Skills for Behavioral Change_

## Lesson (course text)

Knowing a skill is not the same as using it. Real improvement happens when you apply learning during actual workplace situations.

Use reminders, plans, or prompts to support new behavior. Afterward, reflect on what happened and whether the approach improved the outcome.

Adjust your actions based on results. Consistent application helps a new skill become part of your regular professional behavior.

## Framework overview

Knowing what to do is not the same as doing it. Learners must apply skills in real situations and adjust based on results.

## Competency Definition

Interpreting behavior within situations

## Competency Statement

The ability to understand how situations influence behavior and to interpret actions fairly, accurately, and constructively. Workplace Relevance Workplaces are complex environments where behavior is affected by deadlines, customers, supervisors, team dynamics, workload, stress, culture, and expectations. Employees and leaders who understand context are better able to coach, communicate, resolve conflict, and improve performance without making unfair assumptions.

## Knowledge

- transfer requires attention and reminders
- results provide evidence
- setbacks are part of learning
- consistent use builds habits

## Skills

- choose an application opportunity
- use a cue or reminder
- review the outcome
- adjust the next attempt

## Observable Behaviors

- Uses the skill in a relevant setting
- Notes what happened
- Requests feedback when useful
- Changes the plan based on evidence

## I Demonstrate This When

- I use what I learned in real situations.
- I notice whether my new approach is working.
- I adjust when I need to improve further.
- I turn learning into consistent action.

## Performance Indicators

- **Limited**: Needs prompting to demonstrate apply learning in real situations and may apply it inaccurately or inconsistently.
- **Developing**: Keeps learning separate from work.
- **Effective**: Applies and reviews a skill.
- **Consistent**: Uses the skill flexibly across situations and sustains it.

## Examples

- After practicing active listening, an employee summarizes a coworker’s concern in a real meeting.
- A learner uses a pause strategy during an actual disagreement and reviews the result.

## Non-Examples

- Waiting for a perfect opportunity.
- Assuming one successful attempt means the behavior is established.

## Common Misconceptions

- Knowing a method does not ensure transfer.
- A difficult attempt can still produce useful learning.

## Conversation Starters

- Understanding: What makes it hard to use a skill in a real situation even after I practice it?
- Reflection or Application: Where could I apply a new skill in my current work or learning?
- Practice or Improvement: What reminder or review method could help me turn this skill into a habit?
- FfTT-TSB 4: Understanding Behavior in Context
- Behavior is shaped by the situation. A person may behave differently depending on pressure, expectations, relationships, culture, environment, role, incentives, confidence, or support. Understanding context helps people avoid unfair judgments and respond more effectively.
- Problem Statement
- People often judge behavior without considering the situation, which can lead to inaccurate conclusions, unfair labels, and missed opportunities for improvement.
- Value Statement
- Understanding context improves fairness, communication, coaching, teamwork, and problem-solving.
