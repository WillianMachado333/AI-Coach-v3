# Understanding Traits, Skills, and Behaviors — 6.2 Giving Behavior-Based Feedback

_Section 6: Communicating About Traits, Skills, and Behaviors_

## Lesson (course text)

Useful feedback focuses on observable actions, their impact, expectations, and practical next steps. It does not attack the person’s identity.

Explain what happened and why it mattered. Connect the behavior to a shared goal, workplace standard, or effect on other people.

Suggest a realistic improvement step and invite discussion. Specific feedback helps the person know what to continue, stop, or change.

## Framework overview

Feedback is most useful when it focuses on actions, impact, expectations, and next steps. This helps the person understand what to continue, stop, or change.

## Competency Definition

Giving specific action-focused feedback

## Competency Statement

Provides feedback that focuses on observable behavior, impact, expectations, and practical improvement steps.

## Knowledge

- timing and setting matter
- feedback should be specific
- impact and expectations clarify importance
- dialogue improves understanding

## Skills

- prepare an example
- explain impact
- ask for perspective
- agree on a next step

## Observable Behaviors

- Uses a clear example
- Keeps tone respectful
- Links to expectations
- Invites response and confirms next steps

## I Demonstrate This When

- I focus feedback on what someone did.
- I explain why the behavior mattered.
- I connect feedback to clear expectations.
- I suggest a practical improvement step.

## Performance Indicators

- **Limited**: Needs prompting to demonstrate give behavior-based feedback and may apply it inaccurately or inconsistently.
- **Developing**: Offers vague criticism.
- **Effective**: Gives specific actionable feedback.
- **Consistent**: Balances candor and respect and follows up on progress.

## Examples

- A supervisor explains that late handoffs delayed another team and agrees on a check-in process.
- A peer asks a teammate to leave space for others after repeated interruptions.

## Non-Examples

- Attacking personality.
- Giving feedback in public when privacy is appropriate.

## Common Misconceptions

- Behavior-based feedback can include positive behavior, not only problems.
- Respectful feedback can still be direct.

## Conversation Starters

- Understanding: Why does feedback work better when it focuses on behavior?
- Reflection or Application: How can I raise a concern without making the other person feel personally attacked?
- Practice or Improvement: Can you help me prepare feedback that includes impact and a practical next step?
