# Understanding Traits, Skills, and Behaviors — 6.1 Describing Behavior Objectively

_Section 6: Communicating About Traits, Skills, and Behaviors_

## Lesson (course text)

Objective language describes what someone said or did. It avoids vague labels such as lazy, rude, difficult, or unprofessional.

Use specific examples and separate observation from interpretation. Describe the action, timing, setting, and result when those details are relevant.

Clear descriptions reduce defensiveness and confusion. They help people understand the concern and identify behavior they can actually change.

## Framework overview

Objective behavior language focuses on what was seen or heard. It avoids vague judgments such as “bad attitude,” “lazy,” or “difficult.”

## Competency Definition

Using observable behavior language

## Competency Statement

Describes behavior using specific, observable actions rather than labels, assumptions, or personality judgments.

## Knowledge

- observations differ from evaluations
- specific examples improve clarity
- written and digital actions are observable
- neutral language supports fairness

## Skills

- state what happened
- include relevant time and context
- remove labels
- separate fact from interpretation

## Observable Behaviors

- Uses action verbs
- Provides examples
- Avoids loaded labels
- States uncertainty where needed

## I Demonstrate This When

- I describe the action I observed.
- I avoid labeling the person.
- I give specific examples.
- I separate facts from assumptions.

## Performance Indicators

- **Limited**: Needs prompting to demonstrate describe behavior objectively and may apply it inaccurately or inconsistently.
- **Developing**: Uses vague judgments.
- **Effective**: Gives a specific neutral description.
- **Consistent**: Produces concise evidence-based descriptions across sensitive situations.

## Examples

- “The report arrived two days after the agreed deadline.”
- “During the call, she answered before the customer finished speaking.”

## Non-Examples

- “He has a bad attitude.”
- “She does not care about quality.”

## Common Misconceptions

- Objective does not mean emotionless; impact can be stated clearly.
- Specific descriptions should include only relevant details.

## Conversation Starters

- Understanding: What is the difference between an observation and a judgment?
- Reflection or Application: How could I describe a recent concern without labeling the person?
- Practice or Improvement: Can you help me turn a vague statement into objective behavior language?
