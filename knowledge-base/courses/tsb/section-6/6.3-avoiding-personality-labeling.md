# Understanding Traits, Skills, and Behaviors — 6.3 Avoiding Personality Labeling

_Section 6: Communicating About Traits, Skills, and Behaviors_

## Lesson (course text)

Personality labels can make people feel judged and unable to improve. They often replace useful information with a broad personal conclusion.

Reframe labels into observable behavior. Instead of saying someone is careless, describe the missing details or repeated errors you noticed.

Respectful language supports accountability without attacking identity. Focus on skills, actions, expectations, and situations that can be addressed.

## Framework overview

Personality labels can make people feel judged and stuck. Better communication focuses on behavior that can be changed or improved.

## Competency Definition

Using awareness to improve relationships

## Competency Statement

The ability to apply trait, skill, and behavior awareness to improve workplace relationships, teamwork, communication, and trust. Workplace Relevance Workplace success depends on working effectively with people who have different communication styles, work habits, motivations, confidence levels, and stress responses. This competency supports team effectiveness, leadership development, customer interaction, coaching, and conflict management.

## Knowledge

- labels often overgeneralize
- labels may reflect bias
- behavior and skills offer clearer action
- identity should not be attacked

## Skills

- notice labeling words
- reframe a label
- name an action and impact
- use inclusive language

## Observable Behaviors

- Replaces labels with evidence
- Avoids defining a person by one event
- Focuses on changeable actions
- Challenges labels respectfully

## I Demonstrate This When

- I do not reduce someone to a label.
- I focus on behavior that can improve.
- I speak respectfully about development needs.
- I avoid making comments about who someone is as a person.

## Performance Indicators

- **Limited**: Needs prompting to demonstrate avoid personality labeling and may apply it inaccurately or inconsistently.
- **Developing**: Uses labels as shorthand.
- **Effective**: Reframes labels accurately.
- **Consistent**: Helps groups maintain behavior-based language consistently.

## Examples

- Changes “unreliable” to “missed two agreed deadlines without notice.”
- Changes “not leadership material” to a specific skill or behavior needed for the role.

## Non-Examples

- Using a flattering label as proof of competence.
- Replacing one label with another vague label.

## Common Misconceptions

- Even positive labels can create fixed expectations.
- Avoiding labels does not mean ignoring persistent behavior.

## Conversation Starters

- Understanding: Why can personality labels make feedback less useful?
- Reflection or Application: What label might I be using when I should describe a behavior instead?
- Practice or Improvement: How can I respond when other people use labels in a team discussion?
