# Understanding Traits, Skills, and Behaviors — 5.2 Pausing Before Acting

_Section 5: Managing Traits Through Professional Behavior_

## Lesson (course text)

A pause creates space between an impulse and your response. That space helps you think about consequences before speaking or acting.

During pressure, take a breath, ask for a moment, or review your words. A brief pause can prevent an unhelpful comment or decision.

Pausing supports intentional behavior. It allows you to choose a response that fits the situation and protects professional relationships.

## Framework overview

A pause creates space between impulse and action. This helps people choose a better response instead of reacting automatically.

## Competency Definition

Creating space before responding

## Competency Statement

Pauses before acting or speaking so that responses are intentional, professional, and appropriate to the situation.

## Knowledge

- impulses can be interrupted
- a pause may be brief or extended
- consequences and goals guide choice
- practice makes pausing easier

## Skills

- recognize a trigger
- use a pause cue
- consider options
- select a response

## Observable Behaviors

- Stops before reacting
- Uses a visible or private cue
- Considers impact
- Chooses words and timing intentionally

## I Demonstrate This When

- I pause before reacting.
- I think about the impact of my response.
- I choose my words carefully.
- I avoid acting on impulse.

## Performance Indicators

- **Limited**: Needs prompting to demonstrate pause before acting and may apply it inaccurately or inconsistently.
- **Developing**: Reacts immediately.
- **Effective**: Uses a pause in common pressure situations.
- **Consistent**: Creates reliable cues and adapts pause length to risk.

## Examples

- A worker drafts an angry email but waits and revises it before sending.
- A student counts slowly before responding to a challenging comment.

## Non-Examples

- Using a pause to avoid every difficult conversation.
- Staying silent without returning to the issue.

## Common Misconceptions

- Pausing is an active choice, not weakness.
- A pause should support a response, not permanent avoidance.

## Conversation Starters

- Understanding: Why can a short pause change the quality of a response?
- Reflection or Application: When am I most likely to react before thinking?
- Practice or Improvement: What pause cue could I use in the moment without drawing attention?
