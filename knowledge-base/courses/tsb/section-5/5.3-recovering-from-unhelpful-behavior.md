# Understanding Traits, Skills, and Behaviors — 5.3 Recovering from Unhelpful Behavior

_Section 5: Managing Traits Through Professional Behavior_

## Lesson (course text)

Everyone sometimes behaves in an unhelpful way. Professional growth begins when you recognize the behavior and take responsibility without excuses.

Apologize or repair harm when appropriate. Explain what you will do differently rather than focusing only on your intentions.

Reflect on what contributed to the behavior and plan a better response. Mistakes can become useful learning when they lead to improved action.

## Framework overview

Everyone makes mistakes. Professional growth requires recognizing unhelpful behavior, taking responsibility, repairing harm where possible, and improving future responses.

## Competency Definition

Communicating about development accurately

## Competency Statement

The ability to discuss traits, skills, and behaviors clearly, respectfully, and accurately in ways that support learning, accountability, and growth. Workplace Relevance Managers, coaches, educators, and coworkers need accurate language to discuss performance and development. Behavior-based communication reduces defensiveness, improves clarity, supports fair assessment, and helps people understand what they can do differently.

## Knowledge

- mistakes do not remove responsibility
- repair should fit the impact
- apologies require ownership
- learning should change future action

## Skills

- acknowledge behavior
- apologize clearly
- ask what repair is useful
- create a prevention plan

## Observable Behaviors

- Names the action
- Avoids excuses
- Makes a reasonable repair
- Follows through on improvement

## I Demonstrate This When

- I admit when my behavior was not helpful.
- I take responsibility without making excuses.
- I repair the situation when I can.
- I learn from the experience.

## Performance Indicators

- **Limited**: Needs prompting to demonstrate recover after unhelpful behavior and may apply it inaccurately or inconsistently.
- **Developing**: Defends or hides the behavior.
- **Effective**: Owns and repairs the situation.
- **Consistent**: Repairs promptly and uses the event to prevent recurrence.

## Examples

- A manager corrects a public criticism by apologizing privately and clarifying expectations respectfully.
- A learner replaces missing work, explains the plan, and changes reminders.

## Non-Examples

- Saying “sorry you were offended.”
- Apologizing repeatedly without changing behavior.

## Common Misconceptions

- Repair cannot always remove all harm, but it still matters.
- Accountability does not require endless self-punishment.

## Conversation Starters

- Understanding: What makes an apology show real responsibility?
- Reflection or Application: How could I repair a situation where my behavior had a negative impact?
- Practice or Improvement: What can I put in place so I respond differently next time?
- FfTT-TSB 6: Communicating About Traits, Skills, and Behaviors
- The way people talk about traits, skills, and behaviors affects trust, fairness, and growth. Effective communication focuses on observable behavior, clear examples, specific impact, and practical improvement.
- Problem Statement
- Feedback and coaching can become unfair or unhelpful when people use labels, assumptions, or vague comments instead of describing observable behavior.
- Value Statement
- Clear behavior-based communication improves feedback, coaching, performance conversations, teamwork, and professional development.
