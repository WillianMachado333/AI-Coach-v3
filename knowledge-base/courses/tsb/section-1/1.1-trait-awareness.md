# Understanding Traits, Skills, and Behaviors — 1.1 Trait Awareness

_Section 1: Understanding Traits, Skills, and Behaviors_

## Lesson (course text)

Traits are patterns in how you tend to think, feel, communicate, and respond. They influence your natural preferences across many situations.

A trait is a tendency, not a guaranteed outcome. You can manage how your traits appear through awareness, skills, and deliberate choices.

Training may not quickly change your personality. However, experience and reflection can shape traits gradually and help you use them more effectively.

## Framework overview

Traits influence how people tend to think, feel, communicate, and respond. Understanding traits helps people recognize natural tendencies without treating them as fixed excuses or permanent limitations.

## Competency Definition

Understanding stable personal tendencies

## Competency Statement

Recognizes traits as relatively stable patterns that influence how a person tends to think, feel, communicate, and respond across situations.

## Knowledge

- Traits are patterns rather than guarantees.
- Traits often show consistency across time and situations.
- Experience, roles, culture, and context can affect how traits are expressed.
- Trait assessments describe tendencies and should not be treated as fixed labels.

## Skills

- Identify language that describes a tendency.
- Separate a trait from a single action.
- Explain how one trait may appear differently across settings.
- Use trait information without predicting behavior with certainty.

## Observable Behaviors

- Describes a trait using words such as “tends to” or “often.”
- Explains that a person can manage how a trait is expressed.
- Compares how the same tendency may help in one setting and create risk in another.
- Avoids treating an assessment result as a complete description of a person.

## I Demonstrate This When

- I understand that traits describe tendencies, not guarantees.
- I recognize that people can manage how traits show up.
- I avoid expecting training to change someone’s personality quickly.
- I consider how life experience may shape traits over time.

## Performance Indicators

- **Limited**: Needs prompting to demonstrate recognize traits and may apply it inaccurately or inconsistently.
- **Developing**: Names traits but may describe them as fixed.
- **Effective**: Describes traits as tendencies and connects them to possible behavior.
- **Consistent**: Considers context, avoids certainty, and uses trait information to support fair choices.

## Examples

- A learner notes that a preference for careful planning may support quality but may slow action during an urgent task.
- A supervisor uses a work-style assessment to open a discussion rather than to decide what an employee will always do.

## Non-Examples

- Saying, “She is an introvert, so she cannot lead a meeting.”
- Assuming one impatient response proves that impatience is a permanent trait.

## Common Misconceptions

- Misconception: Traits never change. Correction: Traits are relatively stable, but they may shift over time and can be expressed differently.
- Misconception: A trait predicts behavior in every situation. Correction: Skills, goals, pressure, expectations, and context also shape behavior.

## Conversation Starters

- Understanding: How are traits different from choices?
- Reflection or Application: How might one of my tendencies help me in some situations and create risk in others?
- Practice or Improvement: What can I practice so I manage a strong trait more effectively?
