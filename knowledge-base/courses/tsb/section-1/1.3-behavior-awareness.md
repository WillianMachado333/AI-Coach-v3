# Understanding Traits, Skills, and Behaviors — 1.3 Behavior Awareness

_Section 1: Understanding Traits, Skills, and Behaviors_

## Lesson (course text)

Behavior is what you actually say or do in a specific situation. Other people can usually observe or describe it.

Your behavior may change with pressure, motivation, expectations, relationships, or available skills. One action does not fully describe who you are.

Focus on observable actions before making judgments. Consider the situation and separate what happened from assumptions about personality or intent.

## Framework overview

Behavior is what a person actually does. It is observable, situation-based, and influenced by traits, skills, expectations, pressure, motivation, relationships, and environment.

## Competency Definition

Recognizing personal patterns and tendencies

## Competency Statement

The ability to recognize personal tendencies, strengths, risks, and recurring patterns in order to make more intentional choices about workplace behavior. Workplace Relevance Employees who understand their own tendencies are better prepared to manage communication, respond to feedback, work with different people, and adapt to changing expectations. Self-awareness supports professionalism, accountability, teamwork, leadership readiness, and continuous improvement.

## Knowledge

- Behavior is observable and situation-based.
- A single behavior does not define the whole person.
- Behavior may change when expectations, stress, support, or incentives change.
- Impact can be discussed without assuming intent.

## Skills

- Describe what a person said or did.
- Separate observation from interpretation.
- Identify relevant context.
- Compare behavior across situations before identifying a pattern.

## Observable Behaviors

- Uses specific action words to describe behavior.
- Distinguishes a fact from an assumption about motive.
- Considers what was happening before and during the action.
- Avoids turning one event into a personality label.

## I Demonstrate This When

- I focus on what I observed someone do.
- I recognize that people may behave differently in different situations.
- I avoid turning one behavior into a broad personality label.
- I consider the situation before drawing conclusions.

## Performance Indicators

- **Limited**: Needs prompting to demonstrate recognize behaviors and may apply it inaccurately or inconsistently.
- **Developing**: Notices actions but mixes them with judgments.
- **Effective**: Describes observable actions and relevant context.
- **Consistent**: Identifies patterns carefully, explains impact, and avoids unsupported conclusions.

## Examples

- A team member says, “He interrupted three times during the discussion,” instead of, “He is disrespectful.”
- A coach compares behavior in routine and high-pressure situations before suggesting practice.

## Non-Examples

- Calling someone lazy because one deadline was missed.
- Treating an intention to help as evidence that the behavior was effective.

## Common Misconceptions

- Misconception: Behavior shows exactly who a person is. Correction: Behavior is evidence about an action in a situation, not a complete identity.
- Misconception: Only visible physical actions count. Correction: Spoken, written, and digital actions can also be observed.

## Conversation Starters

- Understanding: What makes a description of behavior objective rather than judgmental?
- Reflection or Application: How can I separate what I observed from what I assumed in a recent interaction?
- Practice or Improvement: Can you help me rewrite a vague label as a clear behavior statement?
- FfTT-TSB 2: Recognizing Personal Tendencies
- Personal tendencies influence how people prefer to work, communicate, respond to stress, make decisions, and interact with others. Recognizing these tendencies helps learners understand themselves more accurately and make more intentional choices about behavior.
- Problem Statement
- Many people are unaware of their own patterns, which can cause repeated misunderstandings, ineffective habits, or unhelpful reactions at work.
- Value Statement
- Self-awareness helps people use their strengths, manage risks, and choose more effective behaviors in different workplace situations.
