# Understanding Traits, Skills, and Behaviors — 1.2 Skill Awareness

_Section 1: Understanding Traits, Skills, and Behaviors_

## Lesson (course text)

Skills are capabilities you can learn and strengthen. Instruction, practice, coaching, feedback, and experience all support skill development.

Learning a skill takes effort and repetition. You become more capable when you practice the skill in different situations and learn from results.

Skills can improve workplace behavior. For example, active listening can help a naturally direct person communicate with greater care and respect.

## Framework overview

Skills are learned capabilities that can improve through practice, feedback, instruction, and experience. Understanding skills helps people focus development efforts on what can be built and strengthened.

## Competency Definition

Understanding developable learned capabilities

## Competency Statement

Recognizes skills as capabilities that can be developed through learning, practice, coaching, feedback, and repeated application.

## Knowledge

- Skills are learned and can improve.
- Knowledge may support a skill, but knowing is not the same as performing.
- Practice, feedback, reflection, and repeated use support skill growth.
- Skills can transfer across tasks, although application may differ by context.

## Skills

- Identify the skill needed for a task.
- Break a broad capability into actions that can be practiced.
- Seek and use feedback.
- Connect skill growth to changes in performance.

## Observable Behaviors

- Names a specific skill instead of a vague personal quality.
- Explains how practice would strengthen the skill.
- Uses feedback to adjust an attempt.
- Applies a skill in more than one relevant situation.

## I Demonstrate This When

- I identify skills I can build through practice.
- I understand that learning a skill takes effort and repetition.
- I use feedback to strengthen my capabilities.
- I connect skill growth to better workplace actions.

## Performance Indicators

- **Limited**: Needs prompting to demonstrate recognize skills and may apply it inaccurately or inconsistently.
- **Developing**: Recognizes that skills can grow but may set broad goals.
- **Effective**: Identifies a specific skill and a practical way to build it.
- **Consistent**: Selects focused practice, uses feedback, and applies the skill across settings.

## Examples

- A new employee practices summarizing customer concerns before offering a solution.
- A student improves presentation skills through rehearsal, feedback, and repeated delivery.

## Non-Examples

- Calling confidence a skill without identifying the actions that build confident performance.
- Reading about active listening but never practicing it in conversation.

## Common Misconceptions

- Misconception: People either have a skill or they do not. Correction: Skill levels can develop through focused effort.
- Misconception: Completing training proves skill. Correction: Skill is shown through effective performance.

## Conversation Starters

- Understanding: What makes a skill different from a trait or a piece of knowledge?
- Reflection or Application: Which skill would help me respond better in a situation I find difficult?
- Practice or Improvement: How can I create a simple practice plan for a skill I want to build?
